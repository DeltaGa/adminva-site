How to Be So Efficient It’s Unfair

There are two rules to never forget if you want to be efficient:

  1. Maximize the value per hour of work.
  2. Don’t do work you aren’t paid* for.

*Volunteering and social engagement are naturally benevolent. Thus, they pay for themselves.

Said plainly: if you want to get the best grades, invest in what directly brings them up (aka study time), and stop doing effortful busywork (e.g. managing your calendar).

That said, you can’t just stop doing your calendar, because it is essential to be organized.

It’s a trap, and I fell into it hard during my CEGEP years.

I would waste up to 6 hours at the start of each term reading all my course outlines (plan de cours in French), and around 4 hours per week just to keep up with my emails, my Google Calendar, my planner and my grades.

In total, I had wasted 270 hours, worth at least $4,200, doing administrative busywork instead of reading my books.

At the start of university, I decided it was time to stop.

That is why I built the first version of Cadence, which now helps me focus on what matters to me.

  • Taking care of my cohort.
  • Helping people.
  • Acing exams.
  • Enjoying life.

Efficiency is not about doing more. It is about doing less.

Get Cadence to focus on what matters to you.
